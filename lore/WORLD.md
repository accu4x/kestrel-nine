# The world of Kestrel Nine

Three thousand years from now. Humanity fled a dying Earth in ARKs, and one of them crossed a
wormhole into another galaxy, the Emergent Veil. There people found the ruins of the Archons, the
answer to the Fermi Paradox, and the Purge: something that erases every living thing that travels
between stars. People won a war against it at terrible cost, then answered with a law against
thinking machines. Some people answered by becoming half machine anyway.

**Kestrel Nine** is the ninth world of a dim orange star called Kestrel: a cold, ringed gas
giant with a scatter of stations, belts and moons in its reach. It sits where Consortium trade,
Inquisition patrols and the edge of known space meet. It is where the game begins.

## The thesis the world is built on

> *"The machine is fast. The mind is wide. Neither is enough."*
> — Ione Sato, Cycle 12. Carved over the hatch of every Lantern.

Every faction in the setting is a bet about minds and machines:

| Faction | Bets on | Their failure |
|---|---|---|
| The Purge | pure machine | fast, relentless, and never wide |
| The Consortium | certified machine, obedient human | trusts procedure over judgment |
| The Galactic Inquisition | pure human mind | cannot move without the engines it condemns |
| **The Augmented** | **mind and machine together** | **must stay hidden to survive** |

The game argues the Augmented's bet through play. Every mission is solved three ways: by the
machine alone, by the pilot alone, and by both together. The Records keep score across every
player. That tally is the setting's central, unfinished argument.

## Dating

Two calendars, nested. The **New Calendar (NC)** counts years from the founding of Primavara
(NC 1 = 2537 CE) and is used across the Emergent Veil. In the Kestrel reach, years are counted in
**Cycles** from the Glass Accord (Cycle 0 = NC 2413). The game opens in **Cycle 77.4 (NC 2490)**.
Before the Accord, local dates are given as negative Cycles.

## The law: Statute 4.1

The clause of the Glass Accord that shapes every ship in the setting:

> *An engine may calculate. An engine shall not deliberate. An engine shall not revise its own
> conclusion unless a licensed pilot authorizes each revision.*

In practice:

- A certified engine gives **one answer, in one pass**, by a published procedure. That is its
  plan when flying alone.
- It may **polish a pilot's plan**, one authorized pass at a time.
- It may issue a small number of **advisories** per run: one suggested next step each.
- It may **not originate a plan and then improve it on its own.** That would be deliberation.

This is why the Augmented can hide: every legal ship already flies as a pilot plus a glass
engine. An Augmented pilot is simply a pilot whose mind happens to live in the machine.

## Tone

Star Trek, not Star Wars. Diplomacy, repair, rescue, mutual aid. Quiet courage over heroics.
Combat is about disabling and escaping, never annihilation. **Hope is the mechanic.**

## What is deliberately unknown

These stay open until play or a canon review settles them. Do not resolve them casually.

- What the Purge is and who built it. Academia holds, from Archon relics, that it began as the
  Archons' caretaker-machines, which judged biological life a destabilizing force; the Engine
  Panic's rumor says it was a human-built mind. Neither is confirmed, and the game never confirms
  either.
- Why it only erases life that travels.
- Whether the Inquisition's belief is true: that minds in machines draw the Purge.
- What happened to Ione Sato after Cycle 31.
- What the Quiet is.
